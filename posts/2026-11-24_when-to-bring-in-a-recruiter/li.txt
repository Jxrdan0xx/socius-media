When should a startup bring in a recruiter?

Not always, and not on day one. We'd rather say that up front.

Your first few hires often come from your network. If the founding team can fill those seats with people they've already built with, do that. The trust is already there, and it costs you nothing.

It changes when the network runs dry. The signs we see most often:

1. You're hiring a role nobody in-house can assess, like your first security, protocol or AI engineer.
2. Founders are spending their weeks screening CVs instead of building and selling.
3. The people you need aren't looking, and the best rarely apply to job ads.

Before you speak to anyone, get the brief straight: salary range, equity terms, what success looks like at 90 days, the interview loop and who makes the final call. A recruiter is only as good as the brief.

And when you do bring one in, look for a partner, not a CV feed. Someone who knows the market, pushes back when the spec won't land the person you need, and keeps you informed without you having to chase.
